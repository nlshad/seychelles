Why Ship Cargo by Sea from Dubai to Seychelles?

LCL Shipping from Jebel Ali to Seychelles (Less than Container Load)

For small-to-medium businesses and individuals who do not require a full 20ft or 40ft container, LCL cargo consolidation offers maximum flexibility. Shippers share container space and only pay for the exact volume (CBM) their cargo occupies.

Whether you are shipping 1 CBM, 3 CBM, 5 CBM, several pallets, or individual wooden crates, our weekly consolidation containers depart on scheduled sailings from Jebel Ali to Victoria.

How LCL Cargo Consolidation Works:

  1. Cargo Collection: Goods are picked up from your supplier in Dubai or delivered to our central warehouse.
  2. Cargo Receiving: Packages are verified, barcoded, weighed, and measured for volume accuracy.
  3. Documentation: Commercial invoices, packing lists, and export bills are reviewed.
  4. Consolidation: Cargo is packed alongside other shipments into a dedicated ocean container.
  5. Export Processing: Customs export declaration is completed at Jebel Ali Port.
  6. Ocean Voyage: Container is loaded aboard the vessel for direct sea transit to Seychelles.
  7. Port Victoria Arrival: Vessel berths at Victoria Port on Mahé Island for discharge.
  8. Deconsolidation: Container is moved to the CFS warehouse for un-stuffing.
  9. Customs Clearance: Import declaration is processed via the ASYCUDA World System.
  10. Final Delivery / Pickup: Released cargo is dispatched for doorstep delivery or collected.

What Happens When Cargo Arrives at Port Victoria?

Upon arrival at Port Victoria on Mahé Island, all imported goods are subjected to statutory import procedures governed by the Seychelles Revenue Commission (SRC). Import declarations are lodged electronically using the ASYCUDA World System.

Customs officers at Port Victoria perform cargo inspections, valuation checks, and container verifications prior to releasing shipments for local distribution across Mahé, Praslin, and La Digue.

Mandatory Shipping Documents for Seychelles Import:

  • Commercial Invoice Detailed description, quantity, currency & transaction values.
  • Packing List Package count, gross/net weights, and box dimensions.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) Official sea transit carriage document issued by the carrier.
  • Insurance Certificate Marine cargo protection policy proof where applicable.
  • Import Permit / Licences Required for restricted, agricultural, or regulated items.

Customs Duties and Taxes in Seychelles

Import duties and taxes in Seychelles are determined by the Harmonized System (HS) Code classification of your cargo. Taxes collected by the Seychelles Revenue Commission include:

  • Customs Duty: Variable rate based on HS tariff classification.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): Standard statutory import VAT.
  • Excise Tax: Applied to specific categories such as alcohol, tobacco, and motor vehicles.
  • Environmental Levy: Statutory eco-levy on specified manufactured goods.

Special Attention for Restricted and Regulated Goods

Seychelles enforces strict biosecurity and environmental regulations to preserve its delicate island ecosystem. Agricultural products, plants, animal foods, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, and used timber crating require pre-approval permits and phytosanitary or fumigation certificates before departure from Dubai.

Transit Times & Shipping Costs from Jebel Ali to Seychelles

Ocean shipping transit times from Dubai to Port Victoria depend on shipping line rotations, direct vs transshipment vessel schedules, and port handling. To plan commercial inventory accurately, shippers should request the latest vessel schedule and ETA prior to booking.

Freight rates are calculated based on cargo volume (CBM), gross weight, shipping service type (LCL vs FCL), and whether door delivery is selected.

7 Common Shipping Mistakes to Avoid:

  1. Delayed Booking: Missing cargo gate cut-off dates at Jebel Ali.
  2. Inaccurate CBM Measurements: Estimating dimensions improperly leads to invoice discrepancies.
  3. Incomplete Documentation: Missing commercial invoices or packing lists delays clearance.
  4. Vague Cargo Descriptions: Using generic terms like "goods" or "items" triggers customs audits.
  5. Ignoring Biosecurity Rules: Shipping restricted food/plants without import permits.
  6. Wrong Shipping Mode: Booking FCL for small packages or LCL for full machinery loads.
  7. Unconfirmed Sailing Dates: Not checking updated vessel ETD/ETA before shipping.
